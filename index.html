Search demand glossary: 15 terms a site owner meets

Published by Querytally. We sell search research, so we have an interest in the subject.

These are the words that turn up in SEO reports, ad account reviews and agency proposals. Each one is explained in plain terms, with what it means for a site owner reading the report.

Competitor keywords · Content gap · Head search · Keyword cannibalisation · Keyword cluster · Local SEO audit · Long-tail search · Match type · Negative keyword · PPC audit · Search intent · Search terms report · Search volume · Topical map · Wrong-audience search
Competitor keywords
The searches a rival's pages are built to answer. A useful list is short: one row per need, each with a number and a link to the rival's page. Long exports with thousands of rows are hard to act on. More on reading a list of competitor keywords.
Content gap
A topic people search for that your site has no good page for, while a rival does. Some gaps need a new page. Others need a thin existing page rewritten. A content gap analysis lists them so you can choose which to fill first.
Head search
The short, broad search at the top of a need, such as "roof repair". It usually draws the most searches in its group. It is also the hardest to win, because every rival aims for it.
Keyword cannibalisation
Two or more of your own pages chasing the same need. "EV charger installation cost" and "cost to install an EV charger" are one question. Two pages for it split the attention that one strong page would get.
Keyword cluster
A set of searches that share one need, answered by one page. Clusters stop a site from building ten thin pages where one would do. See how a finished keyword clustering sample reads.
Local SEO audit
A review of one local business against the local rivals that win the calls it wants. A useful one ends with fixes in order, each with a reason, rather than a score. Here is what a finished local SEO audit shows.
Long-tail search
A longer, more specific search, such as "emergency roof repair after storm damage". Each one draws fewer searches than the head. Together they often add up to more, and the people typing them tend to know what they want.
Match type
The setting that controls which searches can trigger an ad or a negative keyword: broad, phrase or exact. The same word blocks or allows very different searches depending on it. Getting it wrong is a common reason a negative list blocks buyers.
Negative keyword
A search you tell your ads never to show for. A good negative is written as a whole search, not a single word. A list made for one market rarely fits another.
PPC audit
A review of a paid search account that lists what costs money without bringing customers. The useful ones put the fixes in order, with the money each leak loses. Here is what a finished PPC audit should show.
Search intent
What a person wants when they type a search: to learn, to compare or to buy. It sets the type of page that should answer. A buying search wants a service or product page, not an article.
Search terms report
The list, inside an ad account, of the real searches that triggered your ads and what each cost. Every line on it was already paid for. It is the main place where wasted searches show up after launch.
Search volume
How many times a search is made in a month, usually given for one country. It is a size, not a promise of visitors. A low number can still pay well if the people searching are ready to buy.
Topical map
The plan of pages for one topic: a hub, its clusters and the pages under each, planned before any writing. Each page carries the searches behind it, its intent and the leading competitor's page for it.
Wrong-audience search
A search that looks like your product but comes from people who will never buy it. What counts depends on the market: "jobs" is a wasted click for a roofer and the whole point for a staffing agency. In paid search these are the costliest searches, because they look relevant.
