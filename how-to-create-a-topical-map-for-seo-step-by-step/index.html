How to Create a Topical Map for SEO, Step by Step

To create a topical map, name one broad topic as the hub, list the distinct needs people search for inside it, group those needs into clusters, and give each need one planned page with its searches beside it. Then decide which pages to write first.

The six steps below are shown on one invented business: a pool service company in Phoenix that cleans and repairs home pools.

The six steps

1. Name the hub

The hub is the broad topic you want to be known for, in the words a customer uses. Not "aquatic maintenance solutions", but pool service.

2. List the needs

Write down each different thing a customer wants inside the topic. Think in verbs: hire, price, fix, compare, learn. For the pool company: hire weekly cleaning, fix a green pool, replace a pump, compare salt and chlorine, learn what pool care costs.

3. Group the needs into clusters

Needs that sit together become a cluster. One cluster is one family of needs, and one page inside it answers one need. Two wordings of the same need ("pool pump replacement" and "replace pool pump") share a page.

4. Put the searches beside each page

Each planned page gets its head search, a few variants, and how many people search for them each month. Without numbers, a map is a list of titles that cannot be put in order.

5. Mark the intent and the rival page

Note whether the searcher wants to buy, compare or learn, since that sets the page type. Then note whether a competitor already has a strong page for that need, or none does.

6. Set the order

Write first where buyers search and nobody covers the need well. Buying pages before explainers. The hub goes live with its first two or three pages, never alone.

Read it from the "Write" column. The first wave is the hub plus three pages: green pool fix, pump replacement and pump replacement cost. All three are buying needs where rivals are weak or absent. Weekly cleaning is the company's main service, yet it waits, because every competitor already has a strong page for it.

The "algae after a monsoon storm" page is small but local and uncovered. Pages like it are cheap wins once the first wave is live.

Common mistakes

  • A page per wording. "Pool pump replacement" and "replace pool pump" on two pages compete with each other.
  • No numbers. A map without monthly searches cannot tell a big need from a tiny one.
  • No rival column. A crowded cluster and an empty one look the same.
  • A hub with nothing under it. Publish it with its first pages, not before.

How big is this topic?

About 590 people in the US search "topical mapping" each month, and about 260 search "topic clusters", one layer of a map. Most of them want what the table above shows: a plan they can build from, not a definition.
